# Interview 2 - Transcript and Salient Points

### Dialogue Transcript

**Interviewer:** All right, then. Good evening, and thank you so much for your willingness to participate and for dedicating some of your time to us. As we mentioned earlier, we are students from Politecnico di Torino conducting a university project to explore how everyday life changes during retirement—specifically to better understand habits, daily rhythms, and especially the moments that make up a typical day. Naturally, we want to reassure you: there are no right or wrong answers, and this is certainly not an exam. We are here solely to listen and learn from your perspective and direct personal experience. We already asked earlier, but to reconfirm: may we record the audio of our conversation purely for convenience, so that we don't have to take continuous written notes? All recorded information will be permanently deleted once the project concludes. You are entirely free to refuse to answer any question and to pause or stop at any time. Do you have any questions or concerns before we begin?

**Retiree:** No.

**Interviewer:** Great. To get started, would you like to tell us a bit about yourself and walk us through what a typical day looks like for you, from morning to evening?

**Retiree:** Sure. I'm Anna, and I will be turning 72 in a few days. I retired from the public administration: I used to work at the Province of Turin, which is now called the Metropolitan City of Turin. I retired in 2016, so I have been retired for about 10 years now. Of course, having worked for 42 years, it was an enormous relief to finally have the entire day to myself. When I retired, my husband was still alive; he had undergone three amputations, so from 2015 until 2021 he was in a wheelchair facing severe complications. When he passed away, I gained full freedom from that point onward. My typical day goes like this: I go to the gym every weekday morning, Monday through Friday, also because one of my daughters works there as a fitness instructor. Then I return home, do some small chores if I feel up to it, tidy up the house, eat lunch, and watch a few cultural programs on television, such as *Quante storie* and *Passato e presente*. Sometimes I might doze off for a bit, depending on how much sleep I got the night before, because I occasionally sleep poorly. In the afternoon I do a few more errands, prepare dinner, and then around 4:30–5:00 PM I come here to the park with my friends and my dog, provided I don't have other commitments. We stay out until around six, and often go for an aperitivo—for instance, yesterday evening we had one right around the corner. Then, once a week on Wednesday evenings, I go swimming. I also hold a theater season ticket with a friend of mine; we attend the Teatro Carignano with the Teatro Stabile season. Now that the weather is getting colder, we will also resume going to the cinema regularly. Having worked 42 years as an official at the Province of Turin and receiving my late husband's survivor pension, financially I really cannot complain. In addition, I am quite a property owner: I own three houses—one here in Turin, a mountain house in Val di Susa above Exilles, and a countryside home in Rivalba. In the summer my routine changes because I relocate between these houses, particularly because this past summer in Turin was scorching hot and I don't have air conditioning yet, so for both myself and the dog, I moved right away. There is always work to keep me busy: repainting window shutters in the country, or up in the mountains I restored a small room with stone paving and wooden furniture. Back when I was still working, I took a regional furniture restoration course.

**Interviewer:** Very interesting. And what other activities or hobbies do you enjoy doing during the week, beyond those?

**Retiree:** Knitting. When I watch TV I knit, because I have two little grandchildren—one is two years old and the other is six months: I've just finished making two little wool sweaters for them. I enjoy it immensely. And then I love restoring furniture; seeing the finished product gives me tremendous satisfaction. In the winter I do it less often because I typically handle it either in the countryside or up in the mountains.

**Interviewer:** From what you are describing, your daily life seems very different compared to the past.

**Retiree:** Very different and very intense—perhaps even more intense than before! In the past, my evenings were perhaps more relaxing because during the day I was at the office, while at home we had hired help caring first for my mother and later for my husband, so I didn't carry as much hands-on domestic burden. Nowadays I am much busier than before, and I certainly do not suffer from loneliness.

**Interviewer:** Turning directly to your social life: how are your interpersonal relationships usually arranged during the week, between phone calls, outings, and time spent on your own?

**Retiree:** Besides my group of friends that I meet up with here at the park, I stay in touch with relatives, cousins over the phone, and friends living outside Turin. Recently, for example, a friend came to visit me from Calabria and we toured all the open-air markets across the city. I am originally from Castagna, in the province of Catanzaro, whereas she comes from a small town near Locri and Siderno.

**Interviewer:** And regarding the amount of time you spend with others, how do you feel? Would you like more company, or do you prefer having your own personal space?

**Retiree:** I feel completely comfortable with things just as they are. Whenever I feel like being alone, I head back home: at home I am on my own and I feel great. I have three children; one lives in Genoa, has two kids, and visits every two weeks. He is an engineer and is about to depart for India for a project, so my daughter-in-law, who is currently on maternity leave, will stay in Turin at her mother's place. She will drop off my two-year-old grandson with me in the mornings—since the other grandmother still works mornings—and pick him up in the afternoon, leaving my afternoons free. So whenever I feel like social company, I come down here and chat; sometimes my friends and I sit together without even speaking. Yet I am equally content on my own: my guilty pleasure is watching TV series. I subscribe to virtually all streaming platforms: Sky, Netflix, Paramount, Prime, Disney. Whatever I want to watch, I find it. That can occasionally be a minor downside, because when I get hooked on a full series, I keep going until two in the morning, and naturally sleep less afterward.

**Interviewer:** Regarding managing your week and time: how do you organize yourself to keep track of schedules, commitments, or deadlines?

**Retiree:** I am extremely disorganized, but fortunately I have my mobile phone to remind me! Just recently, I completely forgot to pick up medical test results that had been ready since September 28th. In fact, I was thinking about booking a check-up at Molinette hospital to see whether I am experiencing any early memory decline, because from time to time I forget details or struggle to find the exact word.

**Interviewer:** So you make regular use of reminders and alerts on your phone?

**Retiree:** Yes, I input everything into my phone and set alerts, so at least I receive notifications.

**Interviewer:** And speaking of sleep: how have you been resting lately? What promotes or interferes with your rest?

**Retiree:** The primary prerequisite for sleeping well is having an unburdened mind. For example, right now I am renovating a ground-floor apartment up in the mountains and need to purchase bathroom fixtures. The initial contractor quote was extremely high, so I went searching through flea markets and salvaged faucet mixers and various fittings. When I have those practical dilemmas swirling in my head, I cannot sleep. Even before taking a major trip, I frequently find myself unable to fall asleep: I get emotionally worked up. Once, when I was due to leave on a trip with a friend, I stayed up until five in the morning, only slept for an hour, and subsequently dozed off behind the wheel on the highway and scraped the car. It was a terrible mistake; nothing like that had ever happened to me before.

**Interviewer:** How do you approach your daily nutrition? Do you deliberate much when deciding what to eat?

**Retiree:** I try to eat properly: I balance proteins, carbohydrates, and vegetables, and I always strive to have fresh fruit and produce at home. Since my husband passed away I have lost weight: I used to weigh 83 kilograms and now weigh around 70, probably because back then I ate partly due to stress. Still, I have a big sweet tooth: if there are sweets around the house, I find it hard to resist, so I try not to buy too many. Every Tuesday evening, my cousin's son comes over for dinner; during his first year of university, he stayed with me before finding student housing. I make homemade pizza, we eat together, and then we watch an action movie or historical and military documentaries that he enjoys.

**Interviewer:** When grocery shopping, do you focus strictly on quality, or do you also keep an eye on prices?

**Retiree:** I aim to spend reasonably, often going to the Porta Palazzo market. My flaw is that sometimes I buy too much produce and run the risk of having to throw something away if I don't consume it in time.

**Interviewer:** And regarding physical exercise, what do you routinely do? Is there anything that makes maintaining it easier?

**Retiree:** With my daughter holding a sports science degree and working as a gym instructor, I go every morning. I attend antalgic back-care gymnastics on Mondays, Tuesdays, Thursdays, and Fridays: I used to suffer from crippling sciatica and lower-back pain that made it nearly impossible to get out of bed, but once I started the gym, the pain vanished completely. On Wednesdays, I hit the weights room: I start with 8 minutes on the rowing machine, followed by mat exercises and dedicated resistance machines to strengthen the musculature—no heavy cardio.

**Interviewer:** How do you feel about living in Turin and in your current area?

**Retiree:** I lived happily for many years in Rivalba in the countryside. Then we relocated to Turin when my mother fell ill and became wheelchair-bound from 1995 to 2002, because managing that in the country was impossible. After she passed away, I would have gladly moved back to the countryside, but my children had their schools and social circles here, so we stayed. Today I am pleased to live in Turin: in the countryside you are entirely reliant on a car, whereas here you walk to the cinema and theater, and you have all the historic downtown cafés and spots right nearby for an aperitivo or lunch. The only real nuisance is downtown vehicular traffic: today, covering a very short distance, we were stuck in traffic for half an hour along Via Principe Amedeo. For that reason, I get around almost exclusively by bicycle: having a restricted traffic zone (ZTL) permit allows me to park locally, but whenever I go to the dentist or for medical tests at Maria Vittoria or Cottolengo hospital, I always take my bike.

**Interviewer:** When an unforeseen practical setback occurs, what do you usually do? Who do you turn to?

**Retiree:** I turn to my children or my friends. Before Christmas, for instance, walking into a shop my smartphone was pickpocketed from my coat: it was an immensely aggravating ordeal given all the bureaucratic steps, and I relied on my children's help. Another concern is nighttime when walking the dog: I feel somewhat apprehensive. Just the other night, two youths on an electric scooter abruptly turned around, perhaps eyeing my gold necklaces; I got frightened and quickly joined a young walking couple, asking if I could walk along with them. For other mishaps, such as locking myself out of the apartment, I left a spare key with my neighbor.

**Interviewer:** Conversely, when there is good news or a joyful moment, what do you enjoy doing?

**Retiree:** I immediately call my daughter to share happy news. With my daughters there is profound emotional resonance, whereas my son, being an engineer, is more pragmatic.

**Interviewer:** Turning to technology: could you share a recent instance where you used a smartphone, computer, or digital applications for practical tasks?

**Retiree:** I use digital services quite extensively: I manage everything through Salute Piemonte and Doctor. Whenever I require medical check-ups, I book online, download reports, and email them to my primary care physician, and she emails prescriptions back to me directly. I also use my computer: for submitting documents or official forms, I scan the sheets and attach them via email. I would say I am relatively tech-savvy: I spent 42 years working in public offices, starting back with the earliest Olivetti computers. Having attended classical high school and university, I have strong proficiency in interpreting complex textual procedures—such as European funding regulations I used to manage at work—and that makes it straightforward for me to navigate digital interfaces and bureaucratic workflows.

**Interviewer:** Reflecting on your life as a retiree: what aspects do you find most rewarding, and which ones feel most demanding or exhausting?

**Retiree:** The most rewarding things are manual projects: crafting knitted sweaters for my grandchildren, restoring nightstands and wooden furniture. The most exhausting aspects are making major practical decisions entirely alone: now that my husband is no longer here, I cannot bounce ideas off him to evaluate whether to install solar panels, replace window fixtures, or sell my mother's former house at a fair price. Having to shoulder all decisions in isolation is the aspect that occasionally induces anxiety and deprives me of sleep.

**Interviewer:** Is there anything else regarding your daily life or health that you would like to mention before we wrap up?

**Retiree:** Aside from some sciatica kept in check through physical exercise, they recently detected a small nodule that we monitor with CT scans and is completely stable, plus a small asymptomatic hernia. Otherwise, I am in good health.

**Interviewer:** Thank you so much for your time and your valuable contribution.

**Retiree:** Thank you. The only recommendation I would leave you with for your research is to diversify your retiree sample widely: I served as a civil servant for 42 years, whereas a close friend of mine worked as a factory worker her entire life and has completely different lived experiences, daily rhythms, and needs.

### General Summary

The interviewee is Anna, nearly 72 years old, a retired civil servant from the Province of Turin who has been retired for ten years. Widowed in 2021 after years of caregiving for her physically dependent husband, she experiences retirement with dynamism, self-reliance, and financial independence. She maintains an active lifestyle: daily morning gym visits for back rehabilitation, weekly swimming, regular visits to the theater and cinema, and routine dog walks in the park with a close-knit group of female friends. She owns several properties which she manages and restores by hand, finding personal fulfillment in manual craftsmanship. Thanks to her administrative background, she displays strong digital literacy and comfortably navigates regional e-health portals (Salute Piemonte). Primary pain points include evening safety concerns in her urban neighborhood, stress-induced insomnia triggered by solitary decision-making over asset maintenance, and periodic forgetfulness that she mitigates by relying heavily on smartphone notifications and reminders.

### Salient Points

1. **Personal Profile, Autonomy, and Post-Caregiving Freedom**

   * **Smooth transition:** Following a 42-year public administration career and extensive years as her disabled husband's primary caregiver until 2021, she views retirement as a restoration of personal autonomy and schedule freedom.
   * **Financial and property security:** Possesses solid financial security (civil service pension plus survivor benefits) and owns three residences (Turin, Val di Susa, Rivalba), allowing her to balance city life, summer mountain retreats, and hands-on maintenance projects.
2. **Active Daily Routine and Physical Well-being**

   * **Exercise as preventive therapy:** Attends morning gym sessions Monday through Friday for antalgic gymnastics (encouraged by her daughter, a fitness trainer), alongside light weight training/rowing machine work and weekly swimming, completely relieving prior chronic sciatica.
   * **Mindful nutrition:** Aims for balanced meals rich in produce and lean protein, shops at the central open market (Porta Palazzo), and monitors portions to avoid waste. Lost over 10 kg after the cessation of acute caregiving stress.
3. **Leisure, Crafts, and Sense of Accomplishment**

   * **Emotionally rewarding hands-on crafts:** Derives fulfillment from knitting clothing for her grandchildren and carrying out conservative woodworking restoration on antique furniture (skills acquired through regional training courses).
   * **Cultural engagement and media consumption:** Follows cultural broadcasts on TV, holds a seasonal subscription at Teatro Carignano, attends films at the cinema, and regularly binges streaming series across diverse platforms, occasionally cutting into her sleep schedule.
4. **Social Network and Intergenerational Ties**

   * **Balance of community and privacy:** Gathers daily at the local park with friends during pet walks and evening drinks, while simultaneously cherishing domestic solitude and personal space.
   * **Close family bonds:** Keeps tight connections with her three adult children; hosts her university nephew for weekly homemade pizza dinners and assists her daughter-in-law with childcare for her toddler grandson.
5. **Urban Environment and Community Safety**

   * **Appreciation of pedestrian and cycling accessibility:** Commends central Turin for allowing her to reach theaters, cultural spots, and medical appointments on foot or by bicycle, bypassing downtown automobile congestion.
   * **Nighttime vulnerability concerns:** Experiences safety anxiety when walking her dog after dark due to past suspicious encounters with individuals on scooters, responding proactively by pairing up with passing pedestrians.
6. **Digital Competence and External Memory Aids**

   * **High digital literacy:** Independently operates email, scanners, and public health portals (Salute Piemonte) to schedule appointments and exchange medical records with doctors, aided by her background in analyzing complex institutional documentation.
   * **Smartphone as cognitive backup:** Acknowledges occasional absentmindedness (e.g., delays picking up clinical test results) and actively relies on calendar alerts and push notifications on her smartphone as an external memory system.
7. **Pain Points and Sources of Worry**

   * **Solitary administrative burden:** Her most taxing pressure stems from navigating major property, legal, and financial decisions (solar panels, structural repairs, real estate sales) without a partner to consult.
   * **Cognitive insomnia:** Pre-travel excitement and unresolved logistical problems trigger acute sleeplessness, periodically resulting in severe daytime drowsiness.
