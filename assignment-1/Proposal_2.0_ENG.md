### b. Themes from Thematic Analysis with Key Quotes

We performed a thematic analysis across all five interviews. The following 5 themes emerged:

#### Theme 1: Freedom vs. Loss of Structure

Retirement brings liberation from work routines but also a loss of external structure. Participants experience this differently: some relish the freedom, while others struggle with disorganization.

- "Not having to set the alarm in the morning was a liberation for me." (Giulia)
- "I don't organize myself during the week, I organize day by day." (Giuseppe)
- "I am extremely disorganized." (Anna)

#### Theme 2: Physical Activity as a Lifeline (and a Chore)

Physical activity is central to wellbeing, but motivation varies. Some participants are highly disciplined; others struggle with laziness or lack of routine.

- "Physical activity is very fundamental for me." (Giuseppe)
- "If I wake up and don't feel like doing Pilates, I don't do it." (Giulia)
- "I am a camel - I walk a lot." (Elena)

#### Theme 3: Independence vs. Vulnerability

Participants value independence but face age-related limitations (health, technology, transport, safety). They develop strategies to cope, but some vulnerabilities remain.

- "I am independent as long as my health holds." (Elena)
- "I don't get stuck [on technology], but I have difficulty finding certain things." (Giulia)

#### Theme 4: Social Selectivity and Quality over Quantity

Participants are selective about social relationships. They value meaningful connections but also cherish solitude. Social life is often informal and spontaneous.

- "I frequent few people because my time is running out." (Elena)
- "If there are worthwhile people, very willingly. If not, I get up and leave." (Giuseppe)
- "I like being alone but I also like chatting." (Giulia)

#### Theme 5: Financial Precarity and Caregiving Burden

One participant (Giuseppe) revealed significant financial strain (600 € pension, 22,000 € tax debt) and caregiving responsibilities for his hospitalized brother. This constrained his choices and led to digital disengagement.

- "I get 600 € pension. take 74 €, I won't pay 272 €." (Giuseppe)
- "I go visit my brother who is in the spinal unit. I have to take care of him." (Giuseppe)
- "They stole my phone. I'm no longer attached to the phone and the Internet (Giuseppe)

## 4. User Needs

### a. Brainstormed List of User Needs

From the thematic analysis, we brainstormed the following user needs.

1. Needs a way to maintain daily structure and purpose after retirement.
2. Needs to be able to stay physically active despite laziness, health limitations, or lack of routine.
3. Needs a way to manage health routines (medications, appointments, meals) without feeling overwhelmed.
4. Needs to be able to navigate the city and access services reliably, especially when transport is disrupted.
5. Needs a way to connect with others without pressure to socialize more than desired.
6. Needs to be able to manage financial and administrative burdens without losing dignity or independence.
7. Needs a way to access information (events, services, health) without relying on complex technology.
8. Needs to be able to adapt daily routines when unexpected events occur.
9. Needs a way to feel safe when moving around the neighborhood, especially at night.
10. Needs to be able to maintain a balanced diet simply and affordably.

### b. Deep User Needs (3 - 4) and Connection to Themes

We narrowed the list to 4 deep user needs that are substantive enough to become the main goal of an interactive application:

#### Deep Need 1: Needs a way to maintain physical health and mobility independently, despite fluctuations in motivation and energy.

- Connected themes: Theme 2 (Physical Activity as a Lifeline), Theme 1 (Freedom vs. Loss of Structure)
- Evidence: Marco's disciplined gym routine; Giulia's struggle with laziness; Anna's gym routine driven by back pain; Elena's walking habit.
- Why deep: Physical health is foundational to independence and wellbeing, but motivation is fragile. A solution must support sustained engagement without being punitive or demanding.

#### Deep Need 2: Needs to be able to manage daily health and cognitive tasks (medications, appointments, meals) without feeling overwhelmed or forgetting.

- Connected themes: Theme 1 (Freedom vs. Loss of Structure), Theme 3 (Independence vs. Vulnerability)
- Evidence: Anna's forgotten medical test results; Giulia's reliance on phone reminders; Carlo's day-by-day organization; Marco's occasional forgetfulness.
- Why deep: Cognitive load and memory lapses are common and can undermine independence. A solution must reduce friction, not add complexity.

#### Deep Need 3: Needs a way to navigate the city and access services reliably, especially when transport or technology fails.

- Connected themes: Theme 3 (Independence vs. Vulnerability), Theme 5 (Financial Precarity)
- Evidence: Elena's frustration with public transport (lines 18 and 67); Giulia's difficulty with Google Maps; Carlo's disengagement from the internet after phone theft.
- Why deep: Mobility and access are essential for independence, social participation, and health. Failures in transport or technology can lead to isolation and reliance on others.

#### Deep Need 4: Needs a way to manage financial and administrative burdens without losing dignity or independence.

- Connected themes: Theme 5 (Financial Precarity and Caregiving Burden)
- Evidence: Carlo's 22,000 € tax debt and 600 € pension; his caregiving for his brother; his decision to stop using the internet after phone theft.
- Why deep: Financial and administrative stress is a significant but often hidden burden. It affects mental health, choices, and digital engagement. A solution must be respectful, simple, and non-judgmental.

### b. Selected Solution

We selected a solution for Deep Need 1: Physical health and mobility.

Description: A playful, audio-guided daily movement companion that adapts to the user's energy level and daily routine. It offers short "movement snacks" (5–10 minutes) triggered by daily habits (e.g., after breakfast, before dinner), with optional social challenges (e.g., walking with a friend or neighbor) and gentle gamification (e.g., streaks, badges, but no pressure). The solution is designed to be accessible: audio-first, large text, simple navigation, and no need for constant internet.

Connection to need: This solution directly addresses the need to maintain physical health and mobility independently, despite fluctuations in motivation and energy. It also touches on Theme 1 (structure) by integrating movement into daily routines, and Theme 4 (social selectivity) by offering optional social elements without pressure.

### c. How and Why We Selected It

We used the Four-Category Method to evaluate the brainstormed solutions:

- Rational choice: Audio-guided home exercises with adjustable difficulty (low cost, high feasibility).
- Most likely to delight: Gamified daily movement challenges with social elements (playful, engaging).
- Darling: "Movement snacks" triggered by daily habits (simple, elegant, fits the theme).
- Long shot: A buddy system with peer matching (high social value but complex to implement).

The "movement snacks" + audio-guided companion combines the rational choice (feasible) with the darling (elegant, playful). It is:

- Novel: Most fitness apps are designed for younger, already-motivated users. This solution is designed for pensioners with fluctuating motivation.
- Complete: It addresses the need holistically - physical activity, daily structure, optional social connection, and accessibility.
- Playful: It aligns with the theme "Playful Health and Wellbeing" by making movement a gentle, rewarding game rather than a chore.

## 6. Project Name and Value Proposition

### a. How the Team Landed on the Name

We wanted a name that is 1 - 2 words, evokes the solution or the underlying need, and is easy to remember. We brainstormed names like MoveMate, DailyFlow, StepJoy, GentleMove, and Rhythm. We chose:

Project Name: "DailyMove"

- It evokes the idea of integrating movement into daily life.
- It is simple, neutral, and not patronizing.
- It connects to the underlying need for structure and routine (Theme 1) and physical health (Theme 2).

### b. One-Line Value Proposition

"DailyMove: Gentle movement, tailored to your day."

This conveys that the solution is:

- Gentle: Not intense or intimidating.
- Tailored: Adapts to energy levels and routines.
- Daily: Fits into everyday life, not a separate exercise regime.
